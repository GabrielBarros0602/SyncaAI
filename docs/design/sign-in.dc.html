  • Each day shows what’s reserved first, and what’s free of your usable day under it.

What these screens decide

  • The same answer looks the same: sign up, resend and recovery each end on one “Check your inbox” view, built from one template. The server’s sentence sits in a neutral box, the address you typed is repeated under it, and two ways on follow. Nothing in it depends on the answer: no icon, no colour, no tone, no timing. The review bar has the pairs (“new address” / “address with an account”) so you can see they render the same. Each flow keeps its own sentence, as auth.py asks, so recovery never tells anyone to finish signing up.
  • Where the page starts: no field gets focus on load. The page starts at the top, the heading is the first thing a screen reader meets, and the first Tab reaches the email field. Auto-focus would skip the heading, open the keyboard on a phone before you’ve chosen sign in over create, and on the set-a-new-password link it would start in a field you haven’t read about. Focus only moves when you act: to the heading of the next view, or to the field the server refused.
  • The desktop’s right side stays from 1100 up, as three plain lines about what the app does. Nothing in it is a figure: no example day, no free minutes, no screenshot. It’s the same at every step, so it never competes with the form. Below 1100 it goes, and the form sits alone in one column of at most 480px.
  • The time zone is a native select over the zone list, “Region / City (UTC±h)”, with this browser’s zone chosen and marked “this browser”. Under it is the current time there, read from the clock, plus one plain sentence on why it matters and that it can’t change later.
  • Refusals go where you acted. A sign-in refusal sits above the button, with the server’s sentence. “Confirm your address” carries the resend action. “Too many attempts” gives the clock time from Retry-After and holds the button until then. Field errors (an empty field, a password under 8) are caught before sending and go under the field.
  • A link that no longer works is one state, whether used or expired, with the server’s sentence and the way to ask for another. A confirmed address and a set password lead to Sign in, and neither opens a session by itself.
  • Fixed from #28: no invented figures, no development text, no jargon in the heading. Tokens: none new.
