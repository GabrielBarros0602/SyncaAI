What this screen decides

  • Where it lives: Menu → Settings, at every width. The Menu is a bottom sheet below 640px and a panel under the button from 640 up, the same as the approved screens. Settings is a page of its own, one column of at most 720px, that replaces the week. “‹ Back to the week” at the top and Esc both go back, and focus returns to the day you left.
  • Saving by section: the server checks the usable day, days off and block as one request and writes none of it if any part is wrong (users.change_settings). So those three share one form and one Save. When one is refused, its sentence goes under that field, a line above Save says nothing was saved, and the other two keep what you typed. Theme isn’t on the server: it applies on tap, with nothing to save. The password is its own form, and Sign out is its own action. Three ways of saving, each one where the server draws its line.
  • Choosing the hours: two native time fields, start and end, to the minute. On touch they open the platform’s wheel or clock, and on desktop you type “07:00”. Neither lets you type a non-time. The length sits under them as a sentence, “16h usable · your budget each day”, and below that the week: each day’s booked figure, free “of Xh”, and its load, now and after. A change that moves a day up the ladder is named on that day. The server’s three refusals are left to the server, word for word. The form doesn’t pre-block them, so the sentence the person sees is the one the API sends.
  • Days off with 44px targets: seven toggle buttons, each at least 44 × 56, in a grid that wraps (7 across at 375, 4 and 3 at 320 or 200% text). Each says its state in a word, “off” or “plans”, not only by fill. Off is the filled primary, like Today, so the accent stays for the refusal when all seven are off.
  • The block: a minutes field with −/+ buttons of 15 minutes, so a thumb never has to type, and the hours are spelled beside it (“90 minutes · 1h30”). The field can still go out of range by typing, and the server answers that.
  • Password: what happens is stated before the button, as three short lines. The wrong current password goes under its field. “Too many attempts” says the clock time from Retry-After (“at 14:35”) and holds the button until then. Afterwards the form makes way for the confirmation, which says the other devices were signed out.
  • Tokens: none new. Every ground is already a pair in contrast-pairs.json.
