What this screen decided, and why

Week of Mon 19 to Sun 25 Oct 2026, Europe/Lisbon, now Thu 22 at 14:20. Summer time ends on Sun 25, so that day is 1500 minutes and its trail runs longer than the rest.

The day header (#17): each number once

  • Booked is the big number, and it is the sum of the minutes that fall on that day, the carried part included.
  • Free sits right under it, floored at zero and with its denominator: “0m free of 16h”. That is the only place 16h appears.
  • Unbooked left as text: the trail already shows it, and “16h of the day unbooked” gave 16h a second meaning. The trail’s screen-reader label carries the day length (“25-hour day”).
  • Cut: the task count (the rows count themselves), “incl. 1h carried” (the band says it), the 01–07 index and “1440 min”.
  • Over budget adds two things only: the chip “over by 1h” and the load sentence from load.ts. Every level above fine carries the accent: heavy (past your 16h) names a consequence, “No margin for anything running late.”; strained (past 18h) and unsustainable (past 20h) add the unbooked figure, measured against the whole day, so Sunday’s 25 hours count. 18h and 20h are fixed in capacity.py; no setting moves them.

The undo notice (#12): one place for the whole week

  • Pinned to the bottom centre of the window, outside every column: role="status", aria-live="polite". It is always in the page, empty until something happens, so screen readers already know it.
  • It names the day (“from Sun 25”), because in the one-day view on the phone that day may not be on screen. The same region will serve that view.
  • It lasts 10 seconds; Ctrl+Z works while it shows. Moves and restores are announced there too.

Saving (#29) and the server

  • The row stays where it is, says “saving…” and carries aria-busy. A 2px line runs along its foot only when motion is allowed; with reduced motion the word is the whole signal. Nothing turns into a skeleton.
  • The numbers change only when the server answers. A refusal shows the server’s own sentence where the action was taken, and the panel keeps what was typed.

Keyboard (#6): one Tab stop

  • The week is a grid with roving tabindex. Each day is a grid row drawn as a column: ← → change day, ↑ ↓ walk header, carried bands and rows, Home and End jump.
  • Enter opens a row; Tab then walks its controls; Esc closes and puts focus back on the row. E, M, O, C and ⌫ open a verb. Space completes, the same key as the checkbox it stands for. N opens a new task on the focused day. The legend above the week lists every key; it collapses, and touch screens never see it.
  • The ring is 2px of ink: inside a header cell and a row (rows sit 4px apart, so it sits inside the edge), outside an opened row.

The verbs

  • Move (#19) shows all seven days. The ones that can’t take the task say why on the button, and the sentence below names what is in the way.
  • Delete (#21) is immediate, with undo, only for a task that hasn’t started, isn’t done and has nothing ticked. Otherwise the row asks, and lists each reason.
  • Edit (#25) states the correction window before saving: “can go back to 14:20 yesterday”.
  • Tag (#23) suggests while you type. An unknown name gets a notice, a “did you mean”, and a save button that says it will create the tag.
  • Offer (#20) is a button that names the task and the day. It only considers days after today, and the line under it shows the arithmetic.
  • Late completion (#22) puts the day it was done in the line (“done Wed 22:22”), so 56h22 reads as a fact, not a glitch.

Tokens

  • No new token and no new pair. The new surfaces fall on grounds already in the list: the notice is on-primary on primary, the tag list ink on raised, hover and tag, the new-tag notice ink-2 on sunken, and an opened row in an overbooked day uses alert-open.
  • The review bar measures every text element against the ground it actually sits on, in the current theme, after every change.
