What this screen decides

  • Moving between days: a bottom bar, “‹ Wed 21 · + New task · Fri 23 ›”, always on screen and in thumb reach, with the neighbouring days named. I rejected three alternatives. A swipe-only carousel fails 2.5.1 and gives no clue that it exists. Arrows in the day header sit far from the thumb and next to the week arrows. The strip as the only way to move has no room left at 200% text. Swiping the day still works, as a touch-only shortcut. With reduced motion, the day changes without sliding.
  • Week at a glance (#9): always visible, as a strip under the header. Seven 44px cells fit in 320px. Each cell shows the weekday, the date, the reserved figure and a track sized to the day’s length. Every level above fine gets the alert ground and alert figure, so an over-budget day shows from across the room. Today’s date sits on a filled disc, and the selected day is ringed. I didn’t put it behind a button, because that would hide the over-budget day, which is the one thing the strip exists to show. At 200% text the strip wraps into rows instead of scrolling sideways.
  • Undo notice (#12): one status region stacked above the day bar, not over it, so New task and the day arrows stay reachable. It belongs to the week, not the day. It says which day it means (“from Thursday 22”) and offers to go there. The same place carries “moved to” and the accepted offer. It lasts 9 seconds and pauses while touched or focused.
  • Header (#11): the logo, Today and Menu, then the week (‹ range ›). The range opens “Go to a date” (#30). The time zone is a sentence under the strip, always visible, because it decides which day a task lands on. The menu repeats it next to the e-mail and Sign out.
  • Breakpoints: below 640px, one day plus the strip. From 640px to 1099px, one day next to the week as a column of seven rows (see 768, and 667 landscape). From 1100px, the desktop week. 640 is where a 220px column still leaves a day about 400px wide, with titles on one line. I didn’t put two or three days at 768: at roughly 250px each, titles wrap three lines deep, the open row and the offer get cramped, and today can still slide out of view (#10). 1100 is where seven columns of at least 140px fit.
  • Forms open as a full sheet with Save at the top. The keyboard covers the bottom, so Save stays visible, and the field in use scrolls to just above the keyboard. Server refusals (409, 422) appear under Start, word for word. Saving keeps the sheet open, reads “Saving…” and sets aria-busy. Nothing turns into a skeleton.
  • Checklist reorder (2.5.7): “Reorder” turns each item into ↑ / ↓ / ✕ buttons of 44px, and each move is announced. Nothing needs a drag.
  • One new token: --alert-solid-pressed, for the pressed “Delete for good”. Light #861a14, with on-alert #ffffff at 9.67:1. Dark #ff9e85, with on-alert #121110 at 9.41:1. It is added to contrast-pairs.json under on-alert. The heavy level now uses the accent on desktop too, as this brief asks.
